The punishment for Qatl-e-amd (intentional murder) in Pakistan is primarily outlined in **Section 302 of the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC), 1860**.

It prescribes three main categories of punishment, depending on the circumstances and the type of proof available:

1.  **Qisas (Retribution):** The primary punishment for Qatl-e-amd is **death as Qisas**. Qisas means "punishment by causing similar hurt or Qatl at the same part of the body of the convict as he has caused to the victim or by causing his death if he has committed Qatl-e-amd, in exercise of the right of the victim or a wali (heir of the victim, or the provincial government if there is no heir)".
    *   If the case falls under this clause, and the proof in the forms specified in Section 304 PPC is available, the court has no discretion but to hand down the death sentence.

2.  **Ta'zir (Discretionary Punishment):** If the proof in the forms specified in Section 304 PPC is not available, the offender may be **punished with death or imprisonment for life as Ta'zir**, having regard to the facts and circumstances of the case. This gives the court discretion in awarding the punishment.

3.  **Imprisonment up to 25 years:** Where, according to the Injunctions of Islam, the punishment of Qisas is not applicable, the offender may be **punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to twenty-five years**.
    *   A proviso to Section 302 states that this clause (c) shall not apply where the principle of *fasad-fil-arz* (mischief on earth) is attracted; in such cases, only clause (a) or (b) will apply.
    *   In situations like honour killings, even if the heirs pardon the killer (who is often a family member), the court can still impose a minimum 25-year imprisonment as Ta'zir.

It's important to note that the quantum of sentence can vary based on extenuating or aggravating circumstances. For instance, the cold-blooded murder of one's parents can be considered an extreme aggravating circumstance, leading to consecutive life imprisonments. Conversely, in appropriate cases with extenuating circumstances, courts retain the discretion to award a lesser punishment of imprisonment for life instead of death.

This is a complex area of law, and the specifics of each case greatly influence the eventual punishment.
